# Vaelora: The Last Listening — Story Seed
**Status:** proposed; to be researched/refined October 16–18.

**Logline:** When a town's protective Spire starts forgetting its own notes, a newly bonded Wayfarer discovers that a proposed cure would protect civilization by shackling every living Kith to a single enforced melody. To save the world, the Wayfarer must help six divided cultures listen to one another without making them sing in unison.

## Opening — The Bell That Forgot a Child
At Bellhaven's Founder's Chime festival, the Spire's lower prong falls silent. Townsfolk begin losing memories: signs go blank and neighbors become strangers. A child forgets a lullaby sung that morning. A splinter below the Spire bears a strange symbol: a **rest** rather than a glyph. The Wayfarer's imperfect Lantern lens detects a frequency between notes.

The player investigates an ancient relay beneath the market, frees a Kith from a broken clamp and faces a local trust choice about exposing the safety network's fragility.

## Key cast
- **The Wayfarer:** customizable player, influential through choices and alliances, not a bloodline prophecy.
- **Maera Vell:** glazier who helped create knowledge later abused for Null-Chains; mentor/crafter.
- **Tovan Rusk:** former Synod student whose technical expertise conflicts with concealed guilt.
- **Liora Vale:** undecided role; likely veteran Wayfarer mentor or default character template.
- **Inquisitor Sol Veyr:** offers compulsory harmony as an apparently rational alternative to societal collapse.
- **Canon-Marshal Iseult Thren:** transport and Amalgam specialist scarred by an earlier voluntary Kith departure.
- **Sister Arune:** moonpool memory-keeper who complicates the ethics of revealing private memories.

## Dramatic promise
The Hush is imposed muting, **not** ordinary silence; rests are essential to music. Villains should have intelligible motives without justifying enslavement. Townspeople, artisans and Kith share agency in restoring safety. A saved world cannot depend on permanent coerced control.
