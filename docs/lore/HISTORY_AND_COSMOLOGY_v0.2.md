# VAELORA — History, Cosmology and the Hush
**Day 1 / 2026-10-16 / Design draft v0.2**  
**Authority:** user-established laws in `docs/CANON.md` outrank this document. All new historical events, names and mechanisms below are **proposals pending approval**. No claims of actual archaeological or scientific fact.

## Dramatic north star
**The world was not created by a single perfect note. It exists because different voices learned when to sound, when to listen, and when to rest.** The Brass Synod can stop the Hush for a time, but only by stripping living beings of their freedom to disagree. The Wayfarer must learn how to build safety without forced unity. The game is not an allegory in which all authority is evil: ordinary engineers, parents and workers have rational reasons to fear losing the Spires.

## 1. Ontology: a playable physics of resonance
**The First Chorus** is the earliest surviving account of how matter acquired stable form. It describes countless autonomous resonances finding repeating relationships. Matter is *frozen music* in the literal rules of Vaelora; stable patterns persist when notes interact constructively. The Chorus is a process, not a god, not a character to defeat, and not a magic song that can rewrite anything without cost.

**Kith** are sentient, mobile melodies. A Kith may willingly gather a temporary shell from the materials available in its surroundings. Its shell's strength, weight, resistance and visual character are material-dependent. A shell may be shattered without killing the Kith, but violent Decoupling stresses the active Glass Plate and creates Dissonance. A Kith may decline a bond, leave after a broken promise, or prefer one material to another. Its consent is not a collectible rarity statistic.

**Rests** are intervals of silence between sounds. They allow memory, renewal and choice. They are not intrinsically corrupting. A town has healthy hushrooms, quiet ceremonies and listening gardens. This is crucial: **the Hush** is not absence of sound; it is an *externally imposed cancellation pattern* that prevents a living resonance from re-establishing its own next note. The distinction can be demonstrated in play: in a healthy quiet room the Lantern's wick continues to pulse gently; in a Hush field its pulse freezes, Kith cannot answer, and mechanical devices repeat one involuntary beat.

**The Seventh Interval** is a proposed name for a lost *technique of negotiated silence*, not a secret seventh elemental affinity. The six established affinities remain Verdant, Relic, Zephyr, Umbra, Abyssal and Cinder. The Interval lets multiple incompatible resonances take turns instead of merging into a single compulsory chord. Its history has been fragmented and reinterpreted by six cultures. It is neither a new combat element nor a button that trivializes every puzzle.

**Bastion Spires** broadcast stable counter-patterns that interrupt the Hush and shelter towns. They require calibrated lenses, Kith volunteers, human maintenance, stored resonance and trade between settlements. They can fail through neglect, sabotage, fatigue, or synchronization overreach. An old Spire can be repaired; a Synod retrofit can function efficiently but narrows its tolerance for individual variation. Spire protection is therefore a civic infrastructure problem, not an instant spell.

**Glass Plates and the Lantern:** a bonded Kith is represented through a plate and may be active only as part of a maximum three-Plate Triad. Other plates remain dormant in the Glyph Satchel. Triads are changed at Chime Hearths. Liquid Echo fuels demanding environmental actions and refills through resonant activity; the player cannot solve everything by spamming abilities. Shell-Grafting occurs at Hearths and respects available materials. Crucible Awakening requires a full eight-note bond, a regional catalyst and a survival rite, never forced fusion.

**Anti-chords:** Dead-Lead absorbs/attenuates resonance and Null-Chains impose a repeated lock-pattern. They can trap Kith in Amalgams; they cannot create genuine consent, duplicate free Kith indefinitely, or operate without fuel, conductors, maintenance and side effects. A Null-Chain's precise limitations will be mechanically specified on Day 3. Freedom must be costly enough to make the Synod's argument difficult, but not so costly that enslaving Kith is the only rational path.

## 2. Timeline — relative chronology, intentionally provisional
All dates use **Years Before the Bell Failure (BBF)**, avoiding an unearned universal calendar. Ordinary towns may keep different calendars. Precise dating is contested; surviving records are fragmentary.

| Period | Approx. date | What happened | Visible legacy / gameplay |
|---|---:|---|---|
| The Unmeasured Chorus | mythic | Stable resonances condense into matter; Kith discover shell-wearing. No single account is universally accepted. | Rare shell strata with repeating harmonic bands; myths disagree in six cultures. |
| The First Listening | >1,000 BBF | Humans learn that Kith can assist in reshaping matter only by willing exchange; hearthcraft begins. | Hearth inscriptions depict hands open, not closed. |
| The Glass Compact | ~730 BBF | Glaziers formalize Plates to safely maintain a temporary connection to a consenting Kith. | Antique three-slot Lantern housings; legal customs around release. |
| The Spire Winter | ~410 BBF | A wide Hush incursion destroys remote settlements. A network of Bastion Spires is built through regional cooperation. | Flooded old roads, wind-scarred Spire foundations and migrant districts. |
| The Concord of Six | ~350 BBF | Six regional councils establish exchange routes, resonance standards and a right of departure for bonded Kith. | Six-signature plaques, disputed ownership of routes, shared festival tunes. |
| The Quiet Ledger | ~120 BBF | A recordkeeping system begins documenting Spire upkeep and missing Kith. Some records are deliberately erased to protect volunteers from coercion. | Contradictory ledgers; ethical mystery around privacy and safety. |
| The Chain Experiments | ~52 BBF | Engineers discover Dead-Lead and emergency Null-Chains during a genuine Spire collapse. Several volunteers survive because of them; others cannot leave the apparatus. | Half-buried test sites; first documented Amalgam injuries. |
| The Synod Ascendancy | ~31 BBF | The Brass Synod centralizes supply, inspection and relay upgrades, initially welcomed for reliable power. | New standardized Spire caps amid older local architecture. |
| The Redaction Years | ~12 BBF | Reports of involuntary binding vanish from public archives. Sol Veyr's synchronized-network proposal gains political support. | Missing pages, censored songs, engineers with divided loyalties. |
| The Bell Failure | 0 | Bellhaven's lower Spire prong fails during Founder's Chime; memory associations fragment locally. | Opening playable incident and first investigation. |

**Causality rule:** the chronology does not prove that Synod created the Hush. It shows that the Synod exploits a real danger and may amplify it through over-control. The original source of the Hush is a midgame mystery. Do not retcon the Hush into an entirely fabricated threat.

## 3. Competing origin accounts
**Shatterglass Reach — The Broken Measure:** a glassblower tells of a perfect bell that shattered because no one could stop ringing it. Lesson: every tool must be allowed to cool. Clue: uneven Spire prongs and deliberate gaps in old bell patterns.

**Sapphire Coast — The Tide's Reply:** sailors say the sea taught the first Kith to answer rather than echo. Lesson: memory is held in return journeys, not in hoarded archives. Clue: tide pools replay sound after the speaker leaves.

**Iron-Weep Deepworks — The Worker's Beat:** smiths believe rhythm was invented by laborers carrying weight together. Lesson: harmony can be cooperation, or the cadence of a chain gang. Clue: worker marks in rails predating Synod equipment.

**Thorn Choir — The Rooted Rest:** forest stewards claim the first song contained a pause long enough for a seed to decide to grow. Lesson: growth cannot be commanded without becoming strangulation. Clue: bridges bloom only after the player waits through a full pulse.

**Aerie of Seven Winds — The Breath Between:** cliff communities believe the Interval is the breath between two weather fronts. Lesson: letting go is sometimes the safest form of holding. Clue: silent wind vanes turn while the Spire's official instruments read zero.

**Still Basilica — The Necessary Unison:** the Synod teaches that early freedom caused the Spire Winter, and only regulated harmony saved civilization. This contains a real truth—uncoordinated responses did worsen disasters—but omits the voluntary agreements that sustained recovery. Clue: identical public inscriptions laid over six distinct earlier scripts.

These are **cultural viewpoints**, not six independent creation events. Contradictions are intentional, and the player can compare testimony with material evidence.

## 4. Hush progression: specific, legible and reversible where appropriate
**Stage I — Drift:** a bell is late, a song's ending slips, a shop sign briefly loses one character. Gameplay: short anomaly sound and subtle visual cue, not an unreadable screen.
**Stage II — Fray:** objects and people fail to recognize familiar associations; some memories become hard to retrieve. Gameplay: NPC route changes, missing labels, alternate quest clues.
**Stage III — Lock:** an area loops a fixed pattern; Kith cannot freely respond and mechanical doors repeat one state. Gameplay: environmental puzzle with counter-pattern and safe retreat.
**Stage IV — Severance:** prolonged exposure threatens irreversible loss of connections between a person's lived memories and identity. Gameplay: major story stakes, rare and telegraphed. Not a random punishment for exploring.

**Limits:** Hush cannot erase every record or memory at will. Physical traces persist; written records are not automatically blank unless their inks depend on resonant pigments. Bellhaven's blank signs therefore should be explicitly painted with *echo-ink*; ordinary stone engraving remains readable and provides a fair investigation clue. The Hush does not delete a person from reality. It disrupts recall and relationships. No plot can rely on a convenient omnipotent memory wipe.

## 5. A grounded opening: the Bell That Forgot a Child
**Player experience, approximately first 20–35 minutes (target, not measured):**
1. **Festival freedom (3–5m):** player chooses clothing and a small Wayfarer background; explores Bellhaven square, plays a chime-tuning mini-interaction, meets glazier Maera Vell. A child, **Nemi**, asks the player to remember a three-note tune. Nemi is an NPC, not the player character.
2. **Disruption (2m):** the Spire's lower prong stops. The echo-ink festival banners lose letters, but a carved stone marker stays intact. Nemi remembers their own name yet no longer recognizes the tune taught minutes ago. Show the loss through a failed shared action rather than a speech.
3. **First objective (5–7m):** find a maintenance key; inspect a cracked lens, talk to two witnesses, follow a sound leaking from the market drain. The player can help a panicked merchant before proceeding.
4. **First ethical encounter (5–7m):** beneath the square, a damaged Null-Clamp holds a Kith shell in place. The player may break the clamp carefully (slower, fewer resources) or cut the relay quickly (damages infrastructure but frees the Kith). **Both routes free the Kith.** Consent is offered through a clear prompt and the Kith can refuse without a fail state.
5. **Consequences (5–8m):** Maera asks whether to disclose the relay fault publicly. Public disclosure triggers repairs but causes temporary panic; private disclosure buys time but damages trust if later revealed. The main quest proceeds in both cases, with changed market dialogue and one side quest.
6. **Promise:** the Lantern now hears a pulse between notes, pointing toward Reedwake. The mystery is not answered. The player receives a modest functional item, not a legendary weapon.

**Opening scene dialogue samples (not final script):**
- Nemi: "You promised you'd know it when I forgot."
- Maera: "The bell isn't broken. Something is telling it not to answer."
- Freed Kith: "You opened the door. That does not mean I belong to you."
- Merchant after disclosure: "I needed the truth yesterday. Today I need a roof."

## 6. The Seventh Interval mystery: three layers
**Public explanation:** a mythical seventh note lost during the Spire Winter. This is inaccurate but useful propaganda.
**Midgame discovery:** old cross-regional agreements describe *turn-taking protocols* that let incompatible Spires alternate load rather than lock into unison.
**Late-game reveal (proposal):** the technique also requires local communities to accept temporary vulnerability, consent from Kith participants, and accountability for failures. There is no permanent, zero-cost universal cure. The final challenge is organizational and moral as much as musical.

This reveal reframes the entire campaign: collect **relationships, repair expertise and public trust**, not six magic keys that automatically unlock the final boss. Each regional Sanctum teaches a distinct cooperative principle and unlocks a traversal/Chord skill. The player still receives satisfying tools and battles.

## 7. Four environmental storytelling examples to carry into level design
- **Bellhaven:** identical festival banners have missing echo-ink letters, while an old stone plaque is unaffected; observant players learn the Hush's limits.
- **Iron-Weep:** a perfectly maintained Synod generator beside a locked worker breakroom; workers' personal tunes have been struck from a shift ledger.
- **Thorn Choir:** a dead garden whose trees all lean in the same direction, unlike the healthy grove's chaotic canopy; forced synchrony has an ecological footprint.
- **Still Basilica:** six local bell motifs engraved under a later uniform bronze casing; dismantling the casing uncovers erased collaboration, not an ancient superweapon.

## 8. Themes to protect
**Safety vs autonomy:** neither is trivial; Synod successes are visible before abuses.
**Memory vs truth:** recovering every memory may violate a person's wish to forget or another person's privacy.
**Labor vs extraction:** Kith consent is mirrored in human working conditions.
**Belonging vs sameness:** regional differences are strengths and sources of friction.
**Rest vs suppression:** silence can heal; the Hush cannot be romanticized as restful.
**Hope through work:** the ending is built by maintenance crews, neighbors and companions as much as by the Wayfarer.
