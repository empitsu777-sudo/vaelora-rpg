# VAELORA — Factions, Institutions and Character Conflicts
**Day 1 design draft v0.2 | Proposed additions, not approved canon.** The Brass Synod and society's Spires are established in the canon; detailed organizations and histories below are new proposals.

## A. The Brass Synod — safety made compulsory
**Public charter:** guarantee that no settlement endures another Spire Winter. Standardize inspections, supply routes, emergency shelters, repair crews and rescue procedures. The Synod has actually saved lives. Its standardized lenses are cheaper and easier to replace than bespoke regional parts.

**Real fracture:** emergency Null-Chain protocols became permanent infrastructure. Kith "volunteers" were gradually replaced by captive resonators; dissenting glaziers and engineers had their reports redacted. The Synod's central network now treats variation itself as a hazard, which worsens Hush vulnerability in some areas.

**Internal blocs (avoid a cardboard evil empire):**
1. **The Continuists**, led by Inquisitor Sol Veyr: require compulsory synchronization; believe one unwilling Kith is a lesser harm than thousands of dead people.
2. **The Wardens**, led locally by safety inspector **Seren Holt** (new): value inspection and transparent repairs, unaware or doubtful of widespread coercion.
3. **The Quiet Office**, managed by archivist **Precentor Dain Orrel** (new): controls incident records and can bury evidence without openly endorsing chains.
4. **The Chainwright Corps**, represented by Canon-Marshal Iseult Thren: transport and maintain Amalgams; its workers are themselves under dangerous binding contracts.

**Capabilities:** logistics, licensed relays, access to Dead-Lead and legal authority in many cities. **Limits:** supply dependence, local resistance, unreliable lock-patterns, divided staff, and public legitimacy. The player can persuade some Wardens; must dismantle coercive infrastructure rather than merely assassinate one leader.

**Quest tension:** expose a Synod relay as unsafe and risk an immediate blackout; leave it operating and prolong involuntary binding. Best solution requires scavenging components, building a temporary volunteer network, and convincing a skeptical inspector.

## B. The Open-Hand Glaziers — makers who disagree
A loose guild of lensmakers, plate repairers and Hearth keepers. **Founding custom:** an open palm is etched inside every legitimate Plate. Some glaziers sell to the Synod because towns need working Spires; others hide defective plates or repair Kith escape routes. Not a unified resistance.

**Maera Vell:** Bellhaven glazier. **Wants:** keep neighbors safe and make reparations for an early lens design later used in Null-Chains. **Needs:** admit that withholding technical truth also causes harm. **Secret:** she signed an inspection waiver after discovering a dangerous tolerance error. **Arc:** from solitary guilt to shared, auditable repair practice. **Player influence:** public evidence vs private reconciliation changes her standing, not whether she can teach Hearth repair.

**Local quest:** a kiln's output can supply medicine vials or new Lantern lenses, but not both immediately. The player must negotiate deliveries and reveal why the shortage exists. Reward: a practical crafting blueprint and changed market stalls.

## C. The Wayfarer Circles — freedom with responsibility
Independent travelers, pathfinders and rescue workers who maintain roads, distribute supplies and accompany consenting Kith. They have no central army. **Oath:** "Ask. Listen. Release." Their decentralized structure prevents mass coercion but makes rapid emergency response difficult.

**Liora Vale:** proposed veteran mentor/default visual template. **Wants:** keep the Circles independent. **Needs:** accept that refusing every shared standard can strand vulnerable villages. **Fault:** she once chose to preserve a hidden Kith refuge while a nearby town lost its supply caravan. **Arc:** develops accountable cooperation without becoming a new authoritarian. A customizable player does not overwrite her identity.

**Quest:** reconcile two rescue groups who disagree on whether to reveal the location of a Kith refuge; both seek to prevent suffering. Reward: route access and improved rescue capacity.

## D. The Tide Archive — memory, custody and consent
Sapphire Coast institution built around tidal echo pools. Maintains oral histories, trade receipts and lost-person registries. Can recover impressions, but **cannot guarantee literal, complete truth**. Records may be biased, edited or withheld with consent. Its keepers fear the Synod's demand for centralized identity archives.

**Sister Arune:** **wants:** protect private memories. **Needs:** recognize when secrecy shelters exploitation. **Fault:** she withheld testimony to spare a survivor, accidentally shielding an abuser. **Arc:** builds consent-based disclosure practices. Player can choose between public evidence, redacted evidence and privately witnessed testimony, with different trust outcomes.

**Quest:** an archive contains proof of a Chainwright crime and intimate details of a victim. The player must disclose enough to stop harm without treating the victim's life as collectible lore.

## E. The Free Foundries — industry and moral compromise
Iron-Weep coalitions of machinists, freight operators and labor stewards. Their machines power hospitals and flood barriers; some depend on unwilling Kith bound in Amalgams. They are divided between gradual transition, sabotage and fear of mass unemployment.

**Tovan Rusk:** former Synod student, now an itinerant technical specialist. **Wants:** prove his engineering can be used without coercion. **Needs:** confess he designed part of a chain-calibration routine. **Fault:** his guilt makes him withhold crucial details. **Arc:** a public repair hearing and hands-on restoration. **Player influence:** whether workers or guildmasters supervise the rebuilt plant.

**Quest:** replace a captive-driven turbine with a cooperative two-source system; requires materials from two towns, a voluntary Kith partner and worker retraining. A quick destructive solution shuts the plant and leaves a lasting town consequence.

## F. The Rootbound Covenant — guardians of living places
Thorn Choir communities who negotiate with Kith and living bridges. They prioritize ecological resilience and consider some Spires too invasive. Their isolation has left outsiders unprotected. The Covenant contains both open border keepers and exclusionists.

**Speaker Fen Oru** (new): wants to stop Dead-Lead contamination; needs to recognize that displaced foundry workers are not personally responsible for industrial harm. **Quest:** build a shared filtration grove with ironworkers. **Conflict:** the grove can absorb pollutants but requires decades of care; the "easy" cleansing song would kill the oldest root network.

## G. The Aerie Signalkeepers — decentralized warning network
Seven Winds pilots, bridgewrights and weather readers. Their warning chimes can reach distant towns without a central authority, but messages are sometimes delayed or contradictory.

**Captain Edda Skell** (new): wants to maintain independent signals; fears standardized relays after a rescue disaster. **Need:** accept interoperable codes without surrendering local control. **Quest:** reconcile two competing storm warnings using environmental evidence and trust in local observers.

## H. The Commons of the Rest — not a secret seventh religion
A cross-regional set of practices: memorial quiet hours, voluntary Kith release ceremonies, open repair ledgers and the right to refuse a dangerous resonance task. Its adherents span all six regions and disagree politically. **Purpose in story:** evidence that people can coordinate without agreeing on every belief. Avoid introducing a conveniently all-knowing ancient order.

## I. Character relationship web
- Maera trained Tovan in optical tolerances; neither knows the full role the other's work played in Null-Chains.
- Liora once escorted Seren Holt through a Hush breach; their mutual respect creates an alternative to violence.
- Sol Veyr and Iseult Thren share a history of real rescue operations; Iseult has seen both lives saved and Kith trapped.
- Sister Arune keeps testimony that could vindicate a Synod Warden while condemning the Quiet Office.
- Fen Oru and an Iron-Weep labor steward disagree about who bears the cost of environmental repair.
- Nemi's forgotten tune is a personal stake, not the secret password to the final boss.

## J. Sol Veyr: antagonist, not caricature
**Public argument:** "A town's safety cannot depend on the consent of someone who can leave in the middle of a storm." He cites real failures and the deaths of people he tried to save. **Blind spot:** he treats personhood as a system variable and assumes every disagreement is a defect. **Plan:** synchronize all Spires with Null-Chains, binding Kith and ultimately regulating human memory patterns to prevent divergence. **Price:** people retain bodies and livelihoods but lose the ability to refuse or create unfamiliar relationships.

**Three dramatic confrontations:**
1. **Bellhaven aftermath:** his inspectors bring genuine medicine and engineers; the player must admit they help.
2. **Iron-Weep hearing:** he presents a truthful casualty report while hiding chain-captivity data; player must gather independent evidence.
3. **Basilica finale:** he offers an immediate stable solution to a failing network; player must have assembled credible voluntary infrastructure to reject it responsibly.

**No redemption by a single speech.** If Sol changes, it is through repeated witnessed consequences and sacrifice of institutional power; otherwise he is stopped, but his legitimate safety concern survives in postgame policy.

## K. Faction gameplay grammar
Every major faction has (1) a service to offer, (2) a real flaw, (3) at least one recruitable ally or mentor, (4) a visible town presence, (5) a route to change, and (6) a failure mode. No faction should be purely good or evil, and no mandatory progression requires pledging allegiance to coercion.

**Proposed trackable flags:** public relay disclosure; Kith rescue; Bellhaven repair trust; Maera confession; Synod inspector persuaded; Iron-Weep transition; Tide testimony privacy; forest remediation; signal cooperation; final network governance. These are **design identifiers only**, not implemented game variables.

## L. Risks for Day 2/3
- Too many factions for a small cartridge: use a handful of named representatives, local faces and reusable faction symbols.
- Moral complexity must not become false equivalence: coerced bondage remains wrong; the hard choices concern how to transition without abandoning dependent communities.
- Don't make every character secretly related or responsible for the same disaster.
- Distinguish six regional conflicts through economics, ecology, geography and interpersonal stakes rather than six identical anti-Synod speeches.
